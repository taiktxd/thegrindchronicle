Before Stephen Curry changed the way basketball is played, he was a young point guard whose ankles kept betraying him.

Repeated sprains. Multiple surgeries. Endless questions about whether his body could handle the physical demands of the NBA.

The basketball world knew he had rare shooting talent.

What they didn't know was whether he would stay healthy long enough to matter.

### The realization.

Most athletes try to fix an injury by treating the spot that hurts.

Curry and his trainers realized something deeper.

The problem wasn't just his ankles.

It was how his body absorbed force, how he loaded his hips, and how he distributed his weight when moving across the floor.

If he wanted to survive in the NBA, he couldn't just heal.

He had to rebuild how he moved.

![Rebuilding yourself starts long before anyone sees the results.](/images/perseverance/steph-core-training.jpg)
*Rebuilding yourself starts long before anyone sees the results.*

---

### Redefining movement.

He spent months away from the spotlight doing tedious, unglamorous work.

Core stability exercises.

Hip mobility drills.

Single-leg balance training.

Re-learning how to run, stop, and cut with efficiency.

He shifted the burden of his explosive movement away from his fragile ankles and onto the strongest muscles in his lower body.

It wasn't exciting.

It wasn't going into any highlight reels.

It was simply the cost of survival.

![Quiet work behind closed doors builds the foundation for legendary moments.](/images/perseverance/steph-weight-room.jpg)
*Quiet work behind closed doors builds the foundation for legendary moments.*

---

### The shift that unlocked history.

When Curry returned with a stronger foundation, everything changed.

The ankles held up.

His stamina became legendary.

His ability to run constantly off the ball wore down defenses, and his relentless movement created space for a three-point revolution.

The NBA didn't change because Stephen Curry decided to shoot from 30 feet out.

The NBA changed because Stephen Curry changed his own body first so he could shoot from 30 feet out without breaking down.

![Before you change the world, you must first change how you handle your own friction.](/images/perseverance/steph-champion-celebration.jpg)
*Before you change the world, you must first change how you handle your own friction.*

---

### The real lesson.

It is easy to admire the finished product: four championships, two MVPs, and the title of the greatest shooter in history.

It is harder to remember the quiet days in the weight room when his career hung in the balance.

Greatness is rarely just a gift.

More often, it is the result of someone willing to reconstruct themselves when everything threatens to fall apart.

Curry didn't just survive his obstacles.

He used them to transform himself—and in doing so, he transformed the game forever.
