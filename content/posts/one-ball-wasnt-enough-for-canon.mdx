Some children inherit a family name.

Some inherit a house.

Canon Curry seems to have inherited something much harder to explain.

A love for basketball that appears long before anyone asked him to compete.

Watching him dribble two basketballs at once, it is easy to focus on the coordination.

But the smile says something more important.

He isn't trying to impress anyone.

He's simply having fun.

### Joy over pressure.

Most kids spend months trying to keep one basketball under control.

Canon casually moves two.

One in each hand.

Eyes up.

Feet moving.

Laughing the entire time.

People immediately see talent.

Children rarely think that way.

To them, a basketball is still just a toy.

And sometimes, one simply isn't enough.

### Growing up around the game.

Growing up around Stephen Curry means basketball isn't something that only happens inside an arena.

It exists at home.

Inside workouts.

During conversations.

On family trips.

Around the dinner table.

The game quietly becomes part of everyday life.

Not because anyone forces it.

Because children naturally become curious about the things the people they love care about.

That is why moments like these feel so genuine.

Nobody is talking about future championships.

Nobody is wondering whether Canon will become an NBA player.

He is still just a little boy.

There is no pressure.

No expectations.

Only another afternoon with a basketball.

### Fascination before dreams.

Perhaps that is the real beauty of the video.

It reminds us that greatness never begins with highlights.

It begins with fascination.

With wanting to pick up the ball one more time.

With laughing after losing control.

With trying again.

People watching the clip may see the son of Stephen Curry.

His family probably sees something much simpler.

A child who cannot stop smiling whenever there's a basketball nearby.

Sometimes the purest love for the game begins before dreams ever do.

For Canon, one basketball simply wasn't enough.
