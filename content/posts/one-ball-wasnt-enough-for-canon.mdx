History has a quiet way of repeating itself inside the Curry household.

Decades ago, a small, baby faced boy Named Wardell used to follow his father Dell Curry into NBA arenas, picking up loose basketballs during warmups and refusing to put them down.

Today, when you look at Canon Curry standing barefoot on the hardwood floor at home, the resemblance is almost uncanny.

The same round cheeks. The same bright, mischievous smile.

And in his small hands, one basketball is already not enough.

![Sometimes play teaches faster than practice ever could.](/images/family/canon-two-ball-dribble.jpg)
*Sometimes play teaches faster than practice ever could.*

### A mirror image of his father.

For years, basketball fans have watched Stephen Curry warm up before games by dribbling two basketballs at once.

It became his signature routine. A quiet ritual of rhythm, hand eye coordination, and muscle memory.

Canon did not need a coach to explain that drill to him.

He simply watched his dad.

One ball in his left hand. One ball in his right. Bare feet pattering against the floor as he keeps both bouncing in rhythm while laughing the entire time.

Most children his age are still figuring out how to control a single bounce.

Canon moves two like he was born with them in his crib.

![Children learn from what they see long before they understand what they're seeing.](/images/family/stephen-curry-training.jpg)
*Children learn from what they see long before they understand what they're seeing.*

---

### The exact same joy.

What makes Canon look so much like young Stephen is not just the face or the two ball handle.

It is the joy.

Before Stephen Curry broke every shooting record in history, people always noticed how much fun he was having on the court. Even against giants, he played with the carefree smile of a kid in a backyard.

Canon carries that exact same spirit.

To him, the two basketballs are not a workout. They are his favorite toys.

He is not trying to impress cameras or live up to a famous last name. He is just a little boy who cannot stop smiling whenever a basketball hits the floor.

![The best lessons are often the ones children never realize they're receiving.](/images/family/stephen-canon-court.jpg)
*The best lessons are often the ones children never realize they're receiving.*

### Growing up in the rhythm of the game.

Just like Stephen grew up listening to the squeak of sneakers in Charlotte, Canon is growing up where basketball is as natural as breathing.

Nobody is forcing him into a gym.

Nobody is putting championship pressure on his tiny shoulders.

He is simply absorbing the craft by watching the greatest shooter who ever lived walk through the living room every single day.

![Growing up around love often makes learning feel like play.](/images/family/canon-family.jpg)
*Growing up around love often makes learning feel like play.*

---

### The league might need new rules again.

The last time a smiling kid from the Curry family fell in love with the rhythm of the ball like this, he grew up and completely broke the geometry of basketball.

Stephen Curry forced every team on earth to defend forty feet away from the basket. He changed how the game is played forever.

Right now, Canon is just a little boy playing barefoot in the hallway with two basketballs.

But looking at those hands and that familiar smile, one thing is certain.

In fifteen years, the NBA might have to change the rules all over again.
