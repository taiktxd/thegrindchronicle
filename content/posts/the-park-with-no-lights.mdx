A mother coming home late from work.

Wanda Durant didn't come home. She returned.

At midnight, after sorting mail for USPS, after cleaning offices no one thanked her for, she would push open the door in Seat Pleasant with her shoulders, not her hands.

She had worked since 4AM. She had every reason to say 'Tomorrow, baby.'

But Kevin would be awake by the window, holding a basketball that was losing air. And she would say, 'Let me change my shoes.'

### A dark park.

The court at Seat Pleasant had no lights. Not broken. Just never installed.

Chain nets rusted. Lines faded. At night, you couldn't see the rim from the three-point line. You had to know where it was.

Most kids wouldn't go there after dark.

But for them, the darkness was an advantage. No one watching. No one judging. Just a mother and a son who had nowhere else to go.

### A flashlight.

She didn't have a floodlight. She had a phone.

A cheap phone with a small flashlight. She would sit on the cracked concrete bench and hold it up, making a small yellow circle around the basket.

Sometimes her arm would shake. She had been lifting boxes all day.

She never put it down.

When people ask how KD learned to shoot with such a soft touch, Wanda says: 'He learned to shoot towards light.'

### A little boy still practicing.

He was too skinny, coaches said. Too skinny to play inside.

So he shot. 100 times. 200 times. The ball would hit the rim and disappear into the dark, and he would run to find it.

He never complained about the dark. Because in the dark, no one could see you miss.

Wanda didn't count makes. She counted attempts. 'That's 87, Kevin. 88.'

She knew talent was not about makes. It was about the attempts you were willing to take when no one was watching.

![88 attempts. No audience. One light.](/images/family/young-kd-shooting-dark.jpg)
*88 attempts. No audience. One light.*

### And she stayed.

Years later, in 2014, Kevin won MVP. He had a mansion. He had millions.

He looked at the front row and cried: 'You the real MVP.'

People thought he meant sacrifice. Double shifts.

He meant that bench. That park. That shaking flashlight.

He meant: You never let me shoot alone.

Wanda still sits on that same bench. The city finally fixed the lights.

Kids ask her, 'Are you KD's mom?'

She says, 'I was just there.'

Greatness is not made under bright lights. It is made when someone stays to hold a small light for you, until you learn how to shine on your own.
