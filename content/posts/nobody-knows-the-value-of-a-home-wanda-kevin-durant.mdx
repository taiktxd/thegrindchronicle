There is a specific kind of silence that fills a house when a father leaves.

Wanda Durant knew that silence well.

Raising two boys on her own in Seat Pleasant, Maryland, she didn't have the luxury of falling apart.

There was no husband to share the late-night worries.

There was no father in the driveway to teach her sons how to navigate a world that often felt too big and too cold.

For years, "home" wasn't a physical place with a backyard and a sense of security.

They moved from apartment to apartment, packing their lives into boxes whenever the rent became too heavy for one paycheck to carry.

Home was simply wherever Wanda could keep Kevin and his brother Tony safe for the night.

She worked the graveyard shift at the post office so she could afford the basics, coming home with aching feet just in time to get her boys ready for school.

Kevin grew up watching his mother carry the weight of two parents on a single pair of shoulders.

In that small, uncertain world, they learned what family truly meant—not because they had a perfect one, but because they had to fight so hard just to hold theirs together.

Years later, basketball changed their lives forever.

The empty cupboards were replaced by sold-out arenas, championships, and financial freedom.

They finally had the resources to buy the biggest house they could find, close the gates, and leave those hard years in the rearview mirror.

Nobody would have blamed them if they did.

Instead, Wanda and Kevin looked back at the neighborhood that raised them.

When they reshaped their philanthropic mission into the Durant Family Charitable Foundation, that middle word wasn't chosen by accident.

Family.

When you walk through the doors of the Durant Center in Suitland today, or watch Wanda hand out warm meals and winter coats to struggling parents, you realize this isn't standard celebrity charity.

It is personal.

Every time Wanda wraps her arms around a single mother who is trying not to cry in front of her kids, she isn't looking down from a place of privilege.

She is looking at who she used to be.

Every time Kevin funds a college scholarship or builds a safe space for kids after school, he is thinking about the boy who once wondered if tomorrow would ever get easier.

![True healing happens when you turn your own survival into someone else's shelter.](/images/family/wanda-kevin-durant-foundation.jpg)

---

They survived the storm, and they made it to the other side.

But neither Wanda nor Kevin could bear the thought of watching another mother and child stand in that same rain alone.

We often measure generosity by the size of the check someone writes.

Perhaps the truest measure of generosity is the memory behind it.

Wanda and Kevin Durant don't give back because they have too much.

They give back because they still remember what it felt like to have almost nothing—except each other.

And if their journey proves anything, it is that the strongest foundations are rarely built from wealth.

They are built by people who remember the cold, and quietly decide to keep the door open and the lights on for everyone else.
